From the studio

Antiquity is just a finish.

Modern Relic started with one question: what would a second-century portrait bust look like if it had been printed last Tuesday?

Each piece passes through the same loop — a reference, a sculpt or a scan, an overnight print, then hours of finishing by hand.

Mostly busts, ornament studies, faceted hardware, restoration work, and one-offs for people who want something with weight on the shelf.

What it becomes

Three kinds of work.

I

Studies

Speculative pieces. Busts, fragments, fictional artifacts. What would this object look like if it had survived?

II

Commissions

A piece for your shelf, a prop for a production, a custom replica from a scan. Send a reference, a sketch, or a description — we’ll work it into the object.

III

One-offs

Single pieces, made once. Numbered, finished by hand, signed on the base. Each one made to be the only one of its kind.

Process

A careful build, not a loud one.

Each piece moves through the same four steps. None of them skipped, none of them rushed.

  1. See

    References, dimensions, scans, textures — and the feel the piece needs.

  2. Shape

    Sculpt or model until the form sits right in three dimensions.

  3. Print

    Resin, FDM, or hybrid — whichever the form actually needs.

  4. Finish

    Sand, paint, patina, sometimes gild. Sign the base.

Good fits

For pieces that need presence.

  • A display piece that needs a tactile, historical edge — without becoming theatrical.
  • A scanned form that needs cleanup or a print-ready rebuild.
  • A product concept that needs sculptural character before it goes to manufacture.
  • A one-off commission that needs to be made once, made well, and signed.
